The Best Time of Year to Build an ADU in Utah

July 31, 2026 · Utah Backyard ADUs
← Back to all guides

Utah's real winters and hot summers genuinely affect ADU construction timelines, more than homeowners in milder climates typically have to think about. Here's how to time your project so weather works for you instead of against you.

Why Timing Matters in a Four-Season State

Concrete work, exterior framing, and roofing all move faster and more predictably in moderate weather. Winter along the Wasatch Front brings frozen ground, snow removal delays, and slower concrete cure times; peak summer brings its own challenges with crew scheduling and material availability during the busiest building season of the year. Neither makes a project impossible, but both affect your timeline and, in some cases, your cost.

Start Permitting in Winter

Here's the move most homeowners miss: permitting doesn't require good weather. Submitting your application and working through plan review during the winter months means you're ready to break ground the moment the ground thaws and weather turns favorable, rather than losing weeks of good building weather to a permit that's still sitting in review. If you're thinking about a spring or summer start, the smartest time to have your first site visit and design conversation is actually the preceding fall or winter.

Foundation and Framing: The Spring Advantage

Foundation pours and framing are the two phases most sensitive to weather. Spring (roughly April through June) tends to offer the best combination of thawed ground, moderate temperatures for concrete curing, and manageable precipitation compared to Utah's snowier winter months or its occasional intense summer storms. A project that breaks ground in spring is generally on the most predictable path toward a fall move-in.

Interior Work Is Largely Winter-Proof

Once a structure is framed, roofed, and weathered-in, interior work (electrical, plumbing, insulation, drywall, flooring, finishes) is far less weather-dependent and can proceed through the winter months without significant slowdown. This is why a project that gets its foundation and framing done before winter can still finish on a reasonable timeline even if final move-in lands in the colder months.

A Realistic Month-by-Month Overview

SeasonWhat's Happening
Winter (Dec-Feb)Best time to start permitting and design; slower for foundation/framing
Spring (Mar-May)Ideal window to break ground once permits are issued
Summer (Jun-Aug)Peak construction season; book crews early, it's everyone's busy season
Fall (Sep-Nov)Good window to close in a structure before winter weather arrives

The Bottom Line

There isn't a single "best" month to start an ADU in Utah, there's a best sequence: begin permitting in the off-season, target a spring or early-summer groundbreaking, and aim to have your structure framed and weathered-in before the first real winter storms.
