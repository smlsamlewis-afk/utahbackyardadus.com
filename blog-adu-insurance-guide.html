ADU Insurance: What Utah Homeowners Need to Know Before Renting It Out

July 31, 2026 · Utah Backyard ADUs
← Back to all guides

Insurance is one of the least glamorous parts of building an ADU, and also one of the easiest to get wrong. Here's a plain-English rundown of how coverage typically works, so you can have an informed conversation with your own insurance agent.

Your Homeowners Policy Covers It, Until You Rent It Out

If your ADU is used by family members — a parent, an adult child, a guest suite — it's typically covered under your existing homeowners policy, though you may need to increase your dwelling coverage limits to reflect the added square footage and rebuild cost. The moment you rent the unit to a non-family tenant, that changes. Most standard homeowners policies either exclude or sharply limit coverage for rented structures, which means a claim involving your tenant's unit could get denied entirely if you haven't updated your coverage.

Landlord Policies: DP-1, DP-2, and DP-3

Once you're renting the unit out, you'll typically need a landlord (or "dwelling fire") policy specifically for the rented structure. These come in three common tiers:

  • DP-1 — basic, named-peril coverage (fire, lightning, and a limited list of other causes)
  • DP-2 — broader named-peril coverage, more causes of loss included
  • DP-3 — open-peril coverage, the most comprehensive of the three, covering all causes of loss except those specifically excluded

Which tier makes sense depends on your risk tolerance, the unit's value, and your budget — this is exactly the kind of decision to make with a licensed agent rather than guess at.

What Landlord Insurance Actually Covers

Beyond basic property damage, a landlord policy typically covers damage caused by tenants (as opposed to the family-use assumption built into a standard homeowners policy), and often includes loss-of-rental-income coverage if the unit becomes uninhabitable and you can't collect rent while it's repaired. Liability coverage for tenant injuries on the property is another piece worth confirming explicitly rather than assuming it's included.

What It Typically Costs

Adding ADU coverage, whether as an endorsement to your existing policy or a separate landlord policy, commonly adds somewhere in the range of $200-$1,200 per year to your overall insurance costs, depending on the unit's construction cost, whether it's attached or detached, and the coverage limits you choose. Get a specific quote before you finalize your rental pricing math — it's a real number that affects your actual return.

Questions Worth Asking Your Agent

  • Does my current policy cover the ADU as-is, or do I need an endorsement?
  • At what point does renting it out require a separate landlord policy?
  • Is loss-of-rental-income coverage included, or is it an add-on?
  • Does the policy distinguish between long-term tenants and short-term (Airbnb-style) rentals?
  • What's excluded, and does that change if the ADU is a garage conversion vs. a detached structure?
This article is for general education only and isn't insurance advice. Coverage rules, costs, and available policy types vary by insurer and by your specific situation — talk to a licensed insurance agent before making coverage decisions.
