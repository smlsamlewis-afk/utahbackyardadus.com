Utah's New Detached ADU Law (SB284): What Changed Oct. 1, 2026

By Sam Lewis, Licensed Utah General Contractor · Published November 21, 2026
← Back to all guides

Key takeaways

  • As of October 1, 2026, most larger Utah cities must allow a detached backyard ADU as a permitted use on single-family lots of 11,000 sq ft or more (Utah Code 10-21-304, created by SB284).
  • Cities can no longer require a conditional use permit for a detached ADU in residential zones, and parking is capped at 2 spaces (650+ sq ft units) or 1 space (smaller units).
  • Cities must also create a process to convert a legally built garage, shop, or other accessory building into a detached ADU.
  • Cities can still regulate size, height, setbacks, lot coverage, owner occupancy, and short-term rentals (under 90 days), and your HOA can still say no to a detached unit.

For years, building a backyard cottage in Utah depended almost entirely on which city you lived in. Some cities allowed detached ADUs broadly, some only in small overlay zones, and some not at all. In 2026 the Legislature changed that. SB284, "Local Land and Water Modifications," sponsored by Sen. Lincoln Fillmore and Rep. Jill Koford, created a new section of state law, Utah Code 10-21-304, that makes detached ADUs a by-right use on qualifying lots in most of the state's larger cities. Cities had until October 1, 2026 to update their ordinances.

If you've been told "no" before, or never checked because you assumed your city didn't allow it, this is worth a second look. Here's exactly what changed, in plain English.

What Changed on October 1, 2026

Under Utah Code 10-21-304, a covered city must adopt rules that permit a detached accessory dwelling unit on any lot or parcel of 11,000 square feet or larger that contains a single-family home, as long as the single-family home is a permitted use on that lot. Cities are free to allow detached ADUs on smaller lots too. 11,000 sq ft is the ceiling a city can set as its minimum, not the floor.

For reference, 11,000 square feet is about a quarter acre (0.25 acres is 10,890 sq ft, so a lot needs to be just over a quarter acre). That describes a large share of older Wasatch Front neighborhoods and foothill subdivisions.

Every detached ADU still has to meet building, health, and fire codes, and the city's rules must include a path for owners of a legally built accessory structure (like a detached garage or workshop) to convert it into a detached ADU, subject to setbacks and code.

Which Cities the Law Covers

SB284 applies to what state code calls a "specified municipality": the cities required to adopt a detailed moderate-income housing plan. In practice that means first- through fourth-class cities, plus fifth-class cities with 5,000 or more residents in the state's larger counties. That includes most of the cities along the Wasatch Front: places like Salt Lake City, Sandy, West Jordan, Orem, Provo, Lehi, Layton, and Ogden.

Small towns and unincorporated county areas may not be covered by the mandate, although many (Salt Lake County's unincorporated areas, for example) have their own ADU ordinances. If you're unsure whether your city is covered, ask us. Checking is part of our free site visit.

What Cities Can No Longer Require

For a detached ADU in a covered city, the city's ordinance may not:

  • Require a conditional use permit if the ADU is in a primarily residential zone. Detached ADUs are now a permitted use, which means an administrative approval instead of a discretionary hearing in front of a planning commission.
  • Require more than two on-site parking spaces for a detached ADU that is 650 sq ft or larger.
  • Require more than one on-site parking space for a detached ADU smaller than 650 sq ft.
  • Impose design standards that conflict with the state's limits on residential design rules (Utah Code 10-20-618).

Parking minimums have quietly killed a lot of ADU projects. Provo, for example, has required four off-street spaces for a house and its ADU combined, a rule the new law overrides for detached units.

What Cities Can Still Regulate

SB284 isn't a free-for-all. A covered city may still:

  • Apply its normal rules on size, dimensions, height, and maximum lot coverage, plus setbacks (which can consider property lines, other structures, easements, window placement, and massing).
  • Require the ADU to be designed consistently with the main house.
  • Prohibit a detached ADU that is larger than the main home, sits in the front yard, or sits in a utility or other recorded easement.
  • Prohibit renting the detached ADU for fewer than 90 consecutive days, which effectively bans short-term rentals like Airbnb.
  • Require the owner to live in either the main house or the ADU.
  • Limit lots to one detached ADU.
  • Require that parking lost in a detached garage conversion be replaced on-site.
  • Deny a detached ADU where there isn't adequate sewer, culinary water, electrical, or storm water access or capacity.

The law also doesn't override restrictions in development agreements a city signed on or before May 6, 2026, so some newer master-planned subdivisions may still be off-limits.

How Utah Cities Are Responding

Because each city wrote its own ordinance within the state framework, the details vary a lot from city to city:

  • Lehi previously allowed detached ADUs only on lots of 14,520 sq ft or more, capped at 1,300 sq ft. In July 2026 it lowered the minimum lot to 11,000 sq ft, required detached units to sit at least 6 feet from the house, and required owner occupancy of one of the two units. City staff estimated the number of eligible lots could grow by up to 20%.
  • Orem had never allowed detached ADUs. Its new rules, adopted in September 2026, allow them on qualifying lots but limit them to one story and 20 feet, keep them out of front yards, and prohibit short-term rentals.
  • Provo had limited detached ADUs to specific overlay zones. This fall it moved to allow them on qualifying 11,000+ sq ft lots citywide, with a draft allowing units up to 25 feet tall. Earlier proposals also discussed capping total accessory-structure coverage at 40% of the lot.
  • Salt Lake City was ahead of the law. It already allows detached ADUs without the 11,000 sq ft threshold, up to 1,000 sq ft and no larger than the main building, with owner occupancy required and generally 3-foot side and rear setbacks.
  • Sandy, Layton, Alpine, Lindon, and Vineyard were among the other cities adopting compliant ordinances in 2026, with their own height and setback details.

Because these ordinances are brand new, city staff and even some contractors are still learning them. The single most useful thing you can do is get a property-specific answer before you pay for plans.

Internal ADUs: The 2021 Rules Still Apply

SB284 is about detached units. Internal ADUs (a basement apartment or a unit within your home's footprint) are still governed by the rules that started with HB82 in 2021, now in Utah Code 10-21-303. In areas zoned primarily residential, an internal ADU is a permitted use, and cities generally can't regulate its size relative to the house, total lot size, or street frontage. Cities may:

  • Require one additional on-site parking space (and replacement parking if the ADU takes over a garage or carport).
  • Prohibit internal ADUs on lots of 6,000 sq ft or less, or where the home is on a failing septic tank.
  • Prohibit rentals of fewer than 30 consecutive days.
  • Prohibit renting an internal ADU if the home isn't the owner's primary residence.
  • Require a rental permit or license, and prohibit a separate utility meter.

Cities that issue an ADU permit can also record a notice on the property, and can place a lien (up to $100/day after a cure period) for unresolved violations, which is one more reason not to build without a permit.

HOAs: The Big Exception

State ADU mandates apply to cities, not to private covenants. According to the Utah Department of Commerce's HOA office, an HOA can still prohibit detached ADUs even where state law and city ordinances allow them. HOAs generally cannot ban internal ADUs, though they can require them to meet zoning and building, health, and fire codes. If you're in an HOA, read your CC&Rs before anything else. Does Your Lot Qualify? A Quick Checklist

  • Lot size: 11,000 sq ft or more is the magic number in most covered cities. Your county assessor's parcel lookup shows it, or we'll pull it for you.
  • Zoning: the lot must hold a single-family home in a zone where single-family homes are permitted.
  • Space: room behind or beside the house that meets setbacks and stays out of easements and the front yard.
  • Utilities: reasonable access to sewer, water, and power. Distance is the biggest cost driver (see our utility hookup guide).
  • Existing structure: a legally built detached garage or shop may now be convertible.
  • HOA: no detached-ADU prohibition in your CC&Rs.
  • Your plans: you're comfortable with owner-occupancy and long-term (90+ day) rental rules.

If you check most of these boxes, you're likely in a much better position than you were a year ago.

This guide summarizes Utah Code 10-21-304 and 10-21-303 and recent city actions as of November 2026. It's general information, not legal advice. City ordinances vary and continue to change, so confirm current rules with your city's planning department, and talk to an attorney about anything specific to your property or HOA.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I build a backyard ADU in Utah?

In most Utah cities, yes. Internal ADUs (inside your home's footprint) have been a permitted use in residential zones since HB82 in 2021. As of Oct. 1, 2026, SB284 (Utah Code 10-21-304) also requires most larger Utah cities to allow a detached backyard ADU as a permitted use on single-family lots of 11,000 square feet or more. Cities may allow detached ADUs on smaller lots, and many already do.

Which Utah cities have to allow detached ADUs under SB284?

The law applies to 'specified municipalities' under Utah's moderate-income-housing statute: generally first- through fourth-class cities, plus fifth-class cities of 5,000 or more residents in the state's larger counties. That covers most cities along the Wasatch Front. Cities had to adopt compliant ordinances by Oct. 1, 2026.

How much parking can a Utah city require for a detached ADU?

Under SB284, a covered city cannot require more than two on-site parking spaces for a detached ADU of 650 square feet or larger, or more than one space for a detached ADU smaller than 650 square feet. If you convert a detached garage, the city can require you to replace the home's parking spaces that were in that garage.

Can I use a Utah ADU as an Airbnb?

Usually not. Under SB284, cities may prohibit renting a detached ADU for fewer than 90 consecutive days, and under the internal ADU statute cities may prohibit internal ADU rentals of fewer than 30 consecutive days. Most cities use these limits, so plan on long-term or mid-term rentals.

Do I have to live on the property to build an ADU in Utah?

Often, yes. SB284 lets cities require the owner to live in either the main house or the detached ADU, and the internal ADU statute lets cities prohibit renting an internal ADU in a home that is not the owner's primary residence. Lehi, Salt Lake City, and Salt Lake County all have owner-occupancy rules.

Can my HOA stop me from building a detached ADU in Utah?

Yes. According to the Utah Department of Commerce, an HOA can still prohibit detached ADUs even where state law and city ordinances allow them. HOAs generally cannot ban internal ADUs, but they can require them to meet zoning, building, health, and fire codes.
